## Added 2026-11-10 (the review)

- **A harmonic-sweep reference for the acoustic F1** (Claude). The references are what limits the F1 question for women: closed-phase LPC needs a closed phase of a few milliseconds and fails above about 450 Hz (D-043), and LPC over the whole cycle follows harmonics at high f0. With the acoustic F1 as the target (D-091), a reference can be read from the sound without either. When f0 moves and the tract holds still, each harmonic slides along the envelope, so the harmonic amplitudes over a glide or vibrato sample the envelope densely. Fit log A(k, t) = S(k, t) + E(k f0(t)), with the source S smooth in harmonic number and slow in time and the envelope E a function of frequency alone; the peak of E near F1 is the acoustic F1. It needs no EGG and no LPC, and works better as f0 rises (wider sweeps). D-077 did the same on KL with the flow known; this is the version for a voice whose flow is not.
  - *Material*: Saarbrücken's low-high-low glides (D-095's dev share, after its allocation), VocalSet val's scales and arpeggios, any vibrato.
  - *Validate on renders first*: KL glides and vibrato, where D-077's measured response is the truth, including with a gap. It passes only if it recovers that response's F1 within a few Hz at women's f0.
  - *Risks*: the source's spectrum changes with pitch and effort, which a smooth S must absorb without taking the envelope's peak with it (the separation is identifiable only if f0 moves enough: state the minimum sweep); the tract moves with pitch (larynx height; sopranos tune F1 to f0), so keep to modest glides and report E's stability across halves of the glide.
  - *Worth doing if* the KL validation passes. It would give D-095 a second reference independent of the closed phase, and the only one above 450 Hz short of a physical measurement.
- **Content-matched before / after comparison, and a recording protocol** (Claude). D-096 measures how far two recordings of an unchanged voice differ; with different sentences most of that will be phonetic content. Outcome comparisons can remove it.
  - *Same text*: the same passage read both times, aligned (forced alignment, or dynamic time warping on the model's own outputs), compared frame for frame or vowel for vowel.
  - *Free speech*: per-vowel-class medians (classes from an aligner, or from the VTL-normalised formants above), compared class by class, so a change in what was said is not a change in the voice.
  - *Protocol*: D-096's curve of error against recording length says how long a recording must be; add what to record (a fixed passage, sustained vowels at comfortable pitch, a glide for the sweep reference, a few seconds of room noise) while the consented data is still to be collected.
  - *Power*: D-096's error with the counterfactual probe's imposed changes (f0 up, length down by x %) gives the number of speakers a study needs to see a given change.
  - *Same-text repeats already on disk*: D-068's USC MRI audio has five passages read twice by each speaker. Scanner noise and AAC make it a hard case; D-068's verdict decides whether it can serve D-096's same-text arm.
- **How much of the teacher's accuracy needs the future?** (Claude; before M5). The student's lookahead is 0-20 ms (plan §7.3), the teacher sees seconds each way. Without training anything: score v3.1 with the input cut off 0, 10, 20, 50, 200 ms after each scored frame (the window ending there), per output, on synthetic validation and dev. If F1 or the length head loses much between 200 ms and 20 ms, the student's budget or its targets need rethinking before it is built; if not, M5's design stands. Check first whether D-050's timing and context probes already separate left from right context.
- **Direct resonance measurement, brought forward from M7** (Claude; plan §6.3, D-043's addendum). Broadband excitation at the lips during phonation (Epps, Smith & Wolfe 1997; Joliveau, Smith & Wolfe 2004; citations from memory, to verify) measures the tract's resonances with the glottis as it is in voicing, at any f0. It is the one reference that owes nothing to the voice signal, so it would settle on one voice what three signal-derived references could not (D-091 point 2). A small loudspeaker, a microphone and a known sweep; one consenting speaker is enough to be useful. Hannah's recordings are private and used only at her instruction. Worth doing if the sweep reference above fails its validation, or for singing above 450 Hz, where nothing else exists.
  - *Pilot sweeps (2026-11-13, Hannah's recordings, private)*: a 10 s exponential sweep from a small loudspeaker, recorded at the singer's microphone position, once in the empty set-up and once while she sustained /ɐ/. Deconvolution rejects the voice well: the response sits 40-53 dB above the voice's leakage at 300-2500 Hz. The /ɐ/ recording's 100 ms response was 5-8 dB higher at 500-800 Hz than the empty one's, but speaker and body positions differed between the two, so nothing can be attributed to the tract. A usable version would keep one geometry and sweep during several vowels (and a closed mouth), reading the differences between vowels; the method proper puts both source and microphone at the lips. The room decays at about RT60 0.3 s; the speaker loses 20-40 dB below 300 Hz. A closer look at the early response (same day): the /ɐ/ recording carries an extra decaying component 4-20 ms after the direct sound, confined to about 560-1100 Hz (about 1 dB/ms, a bandwidth near 40 Hz), with a spectral peak near where the models read the vowel's F1; the empty recording has nothing like it. Consistent with the tract ringing, but one recording and a changed geometry. The design that would settle it also opens a closed-glottis reference: one fixed geometry, sweeps while the vowel posture is held silently with the glottis closed (breath held), held with it open, and phonated, plus a closed mouth; the closed-held sweep gives the closed-glottis resonances directly, with no voice to reject.
- **A known-increment test of the length head** (Claude). A tube of known length held at the lips lengthens the tract by about that length. The length head should move by the increment, in order, across two or three tubes. The MRI set gives truth between speakers (and per frame, `vtl-mri`); this gives a dose and a response within one voice, which is the shape of an outcome. Caveats: a narrow tube is not a uniform lengthening (it changes radiation and adds its own resonances), and no training clip has such a shape, so read direction and order, not exact size. Same privacy rule as above.

Its area function comes from a 2D articulatory model (17 articulators: jaw, tongue body and tip, root, lips, velum, larynx, epilarynx and others) through region-wise A = alpha d^beta, so its shape family is not Story's; speakers are male / female / child bodies and a continuous blend (scale, pharynx ratio, tongue radius, lip width).

## Added 2026-11-12

- **Taking the aVTL / iVTL split further on real voices** (Hannah, after D-100; not checked or registered). D-100: on single Hillenbrand words aVTL follows iVTL almost one to one within a speaker (R^2 0.97, slope about 1). An iVTL head gives no reliability gain between models, and a perfect split is bounded at +0.65 reliability alone and about +0.3 once context is there. Context at inference already lifts every model from about 0.24 to 0.70. Probe 10 shows the same leakage on synthetic clips (aVTL's slope on larynx / lip changes 0.9-1.07 in 5b and D-098; 5c 0.67-0.77; ideal 0), so it is not only a domain gap.
  - *Options discussed*:
    - **A vowel head with an adversary on aVTL**: the weakest fit. Vowel identity and length live in the same formants, so removing vowel information from aVTL's input also removes the formant pattern that tells rounding from scale. The goal is an output invariant within a speaker, not features without vowel information. It also needs vowel labels: tube clips have them; cascade clips (drawn formants) and the resynthesised pools (alignments) don't.
    - **Decorrelating aVTL from iVTL**: on synthetic clips it repeats what the labels already ask (aVTL constant within a clip). On real audio any per-speaker-constant aVTL satisfies it, so it reduces to speaker consistency, with iVTL's own errors folded in.
    - **Speaker consistency on real audio** (preferred, if any): within-speaker variance of aVTL across utterances relative to between-speaker variance in the batch, which is scale-free like the reliability measure. It uses the resynthesised pools' speaker ids (VocalSet singers; Zeroth's numeric ids only, never names). The risk is aVTL turning into a speaker embedding, reliable but not length. Guards: synthetic aVTL error, w/m, the f0 probe, and a counterfactual length-scaling probe (resynthesis at a scaled length must move aVTL 1:1; to be built first, `considering.md`'s counterfactual resynthesis).
  - *Checks before registering anything* (CPU, about an hour):
    - (a) A Bayes ceiling on the training labels: from one static vowel, how much of aVTL is recoverable given iVTL or the formants (D-083: the true iVTL as input took aVTL from 0.155 to 0.125 cm, 0.078 with 2 s of context). If the optimal slope of aVTL on iVTL is near 1, per-word separation is information-limited. Then the answer is context (a session-level aVTL), not a loss.
    - (b) How often articulation moves within a clip under fixed anatomy in train_v2, train_long_v3u and train_v2ku. If it is rare, upweighting or adding such clips is the label-supervised form of the same invariance, with no new loss.
  - *Underneath*: whether iVTL measures articulation at all. Its rounded / unrounded order (D-100) is also what a formant-scale reader gives. D-097's lip-tube test would settle it, and a loss steering aVTL by iVTL is only as good as iVTL.
  - *For the outcomes aim*: a per-recording length from several seconds of running speech is already at 0.7-0.8 (PTDB-TUG, D-100), so per-word anatomy isn't needed for session-level comparisons.

## Models

- **A wider input band (24-32 kHz), distilled down to 16 kHz** (2026-11-08; Hannah, from her own and small-tract spectrograms). Everything runs at 16 kHz, so nothing above 8 kHz is seen. Above it are the upper resonances (F5 and up; a small tract's F6-F8 sit near 6-9 kHz), stable spectral notches that move with the speaker, the aspiration and source tilt, fricatives, and more samples of the envelope at high f0. Not a fix for F4 on clean audio: F4 is in band at 16 kHz even for a 13 cm tract (D-087 tests the related question, F4 removed by the training mix's low-pass). Costs: the training pools re-rendered at the wider rate behind a flag (synth labels capped at 7.6 kHz, tract.py), so 16 kHz rendering and its golden hashes stay as they are and the wide rate gets golden cases of its own; evaluation sets recorded at 16 kHz can't test the wide model. Possibly two model series (Hannah): a 16 kHz one for limited compute in real time, and a 24 or 32 kHz one for machines that can run it. A teacher at 32 kHz with a 16 kHz student is workable: frequency labels transfer unchanged, but the student can't see what the teacher used, so it should learn the teacher's distribution (soft targets), and its sigma needs recalibrating on 16 kHz input, not copying. An F5 output (Hannah, 2026-11-08) is the natural first question for a wide model: whether a head on F5 (and the band above it) helps F1-F4, as the true other formants did in D-084's oracle. Exploratory and expensive; not scheduled. D-087: F4's visibility costs F1 only a couple of Hz at 16 kHz, so this needs its own reason, not the overread. Check first that the band holds voice information on our data (flat lines that don't follow the voice are the room or chain).
- **MERT as a research comparison** (2026-10-26; arXiv 2306.00107). A self-supervised music encoder: 95M / 330M parameters, 24 kHz input, 75 Hz frames, trained on 5 s segments.
  - Against it:
    - CC BY-NC 4.0 weights, on Hugging Face only, so it needs an explicit exception and can never feed anything released.
    - Training data is 160K hours of internet-mined music with no source list, so MIR-1K and MedleyDB (behind MDB-stem-synth) can't be ruled out; it would be scored on Hillenbrand and PTDB-TUG only.
    - Both of its pretraining teachers are acoustic (EnCodec codes and the CQT), so it likely behaves more like DAC than Parakeet.
  - Worth doing only as a quick frozen-head probe, if a singing-trained encoder becomes a real question.

- **Trunk structure: where capacity and cost sit** (2026-10-31; Hannah, after reviewing the encoder for D-054). Teacher v3.1's forward cost, by layer shapes (not profiled): main 2-D conv stack about 27 M multiply-adds per frame (~80 %), the f0 net's convs ~3 M, the two flatten projections ~3 M, the two Conformer blocks ~0.7 M, the heads 0.2 M (MLP heads 1.0 M). The native convs run slower per FLOP than the Linear layers' matmuls, so their share of wall time is larger still. D-054's smoke runs agree: MLP heads with 5.6 × the flat heads' parameters ran at the same speed, while the frequency-resolved read-out (fewer parameters, but conv work at 363 bins) halved it. Candidate changes, each its own arm against the current trunk, ordered by how cheap they are:
  - *Capacity after the flatten*: a Conformer of width 256 and four layers (about +5 M multiply-adds per frame, perhaps +5–10 % wall time), a wider embedding (384 → 768: +2.2 M), MLP heads (about free). Only worth it if something points at the temporal stage; nothing does yet (D-050's stretch, reversal, timing and context probes found no temporal weakness).
  - *A residual or factorised projection across the bottleneck*: the 5,824 → 384 flatten is the one non-residual layer between the conv stack and the Conformer and holds 53 % of the parameters. Compress per channel first, then mix, with a skip around it. The cheap fallback if D-054's read-out helps but costs too much; it gives no log-frequency equivariance.
  - *Frequency (axial) attention before pooling*: attention across the 91 bins within each frame, so distant bands (F1 with F2, a formant with the harmonic comb) interact without relying on dilations; an alternative to D-054's decoder.
  - *A lighter frequency-resolved decoder*, if D-054 supports the read-out: 16 channels at full resolution instead of 32 would cut most of its cost (estimated +25–40 % end to end at 32; the frozen runs overstate it, since there the trunk only runs forward).
  - *Separating D-054's two read-outs*: its film arm changes the formant read-out (per bin) and the bandwidth read-out (at each formant's position) together, under one rule; if it passes, an ablation keeping one of the two would say which carried it.
  - *If D-054's per-bin read-out fails on formant identity* (2026-10-31; Hannah, after D-054's interim result: film seed 1's synthetic F1-F4 MAE about 2.4 × mlp's at step 2,000). A read-out that is the same at every bin has no absolute frequency, so "this peak is F2" has to come from the broadcast context and the conv receptive field; the flat heads get it from per-bin weights, and the cascade mask only orders the formants. Check first, on D-053's frames for film seed 1: if most of the error is assignment to a neighbouring formant (swaps), try, each its own arm on the frozen trunk:
    - *Learned formant queries* (the DETR / Perceiver IO / Slot Attention decoder; dispatch and combine in mixture-of-experts terms): four learned queries, one per formant slot, cross-attend over the per-bin features, and formant n's heat map is query n's attention score at each bin. Identity lives in the query, position in the bins; with relative log-frequency encodings in the keys the read-out stays close to shift-equivariant. No Hungarian matching: formants are ordered by definition and the cascade mask keeps them so. This is a transformer decoder run along frequency, not time (over time the outputs are frame-aligned, so the encoder alone fits, as for token classification; Viterbi in the back end covers temporal structure). Self-attention among the four queries would replace the cascade, which is already causal self-attention among formant slots in all but name (head n sees the expected positions of heads < n); learned attention can also run the other way (F1 using where F2 sits). *After D-054* (2026-10-31): its per-bin read-out failed on clip-level scale misreads, not swaps, so the queries alone may not fix it: they need an absolute frequency anchor too, either absolute log-frequency encodings in the keys or the flat heads' logits as a baseline the decoder adds to.
    - *A log-frequency position channel* in the read-out's input: the cheapest fix, at the cost of strict equivariance.
    - *The per-bin read-out as a residual on the flat heads' logits*: absolute position and identity from the flat heads, local spectral detail added per bin.
    If the error is broad scatter instead, identity is not the problem (look at the decoder's training speed and the logit resolution: logits are interpolated linearly from the VQT's 25-cent bins).
  - *Linear attention or gated DeltaNet for long contexts*: not for speed (attention is about 2 % of the cost at 400 frames, and memory at longer crops grows with the conv maps, not attention, until about a minute per crop). The interest is learned forgetting: a gated recurrent state can reset at a speaker change or a long pause, where full attention carries one speaker's tract into another's frames (D-028; why windowed analysis is the default), which matters for 5d's longer crops; and constant memory per frame for a streaming student. Against: the fast kernels (flash-linear-attention) are Triton, which ROCm under Windows lacks, and a pure-PyTorch chunked version would likely be slower than attention at our lengths; a bidirectional teacher needs a forward and a backward pass (D-050's direction arm: the directions are not equivalent). Gated DeltaNet 2 not yet read. An arm against the Conformer for 5d or a student, decided by probe 5's cross-speaker leakage and the long-clip measures.

## Training techniques

- **A single context convention, with sliding windows at inference** (2026-10-26; from MERT, which trains on 5 s and runs long audio as 5 s windows). - **Lessons from SoFilNet for M4's reconstruction loss** (2026-10-29; Hannah, after reading Meka et al. 2026; `docs/papers/literature.md`). M4 already plans a FormantNet-style reconstruction loss on raw real audio (plan §5.3: cascade F1–F4 + 2 nuisance poles, 1 zero, source tilt, harmonic-sampled targets above ~300 Hz). SoFilNet trains on nothing but reconstruction of real speech, so it never has a synthetic-to-real gap (D-045's perfect separability is ours). What it adds to the plan:
  - *Zeros carry F2 / F3*: without its 2 zeros (250 Hz–4 kHz), its F3 error doubles. Consider 2 zeros rather than 1, with their range confined so they can't take over the high-frequency envelope (theirs drifted to about 5 kHz before the limit).
  - *Losses against blurring*: per-bin spectral losses favour broad formants, shallow valleys and too little noise. Their spectral-contrast, envelope-extrema and band-periodic-fraction losses fix this without labels. Our renderer would need the same if its targets are per bin.
  - *Noise in the renderer*: a per-band aperiodicity term, so the reconstruction doesn't push a noisy spectrum into the envelope parameters.
  - **The F1 risk, which decides whether it is safe for us**: with a flat source and the slope in the filter, reconstruction's F1 is the effective envelope peak, open-phase coupling and tilt included, not the closed-glottis resonance our labels define (D-023). That is the direction of the women's overread we're chasing. The plan's source-tilt term helps; an LF source (Rd) and the coupling shift inside the renderer would make the closed-glottis F1 the best explanation. **Gate before use**: on renders with known labels (breathy and coupled ones included), adding the reconstruction loss must not move F1 from the labels by more than the noise floor; on PTDB-TUG dev, report its shift against the closed-phase reference and R.
- **Pseudo-labels refined by the model itself** (2026-10-29; Hannah; from SoFilNet's F0 targets). Close to M4's re-track → resynthesise → retrain rounds, and to the plan's "gated LP refinement" of candidates; this entry records how SoFilNet differs, for M4's design:
  - *Inside one run, not in rounds*: targets move continuously as the model's filter improves (blended from signal-only labels to refined ones over epochs 20–50). Cheaper than full rounds; harder to audit, since no frozen label set exists between rounds. M4's rounds keep the audit trail; an in-run blend could be an ablation arm.
  - *Refinement by physics plus the model*: the refined target comes from inverse filtering with the model's own tract estimate and then a model-free step (autocorrelation of the residual), which fixed octave errors that the speech's own autocorrelation made. Our analogues: QCP-FB or D-044's R around the teacher's estimates for F1 (D-046: unbiased but scattered; snapping FastTrack to it doubled F1 MAE, so it must be gated on sigma), and the residual-autocorrelation f0 label for octave errors at high f0.
  - *Confirmation risk*: a refinement that uses the model's own estimates can keep its biases. R carries teacher v3's F2–F4 (D-044), so refining F1 that way inherits their errors. Each round's labels need an independent check (closed-phase reference, the identity-resynthesis fixed point; plan §12's acceptance on gold metrics).

- **QCP-FB in the labeller: refinement and ensembles** (2026-10-29; Hannah, after D-046). D-046's arm B (duration quotient 0.3) is unbiased on F1 and resists leaky closure better than closed-phase LPC, but missed its gate narrowly on precision and F3; it is fast and a different kind of model from ours. Two uses to test, each its own entry: (1) **refinement**: snap the teacher's or labeller's formants to the nearest QCP-FB peak within a window scaled by the model's σ (as Gowda et al. 2021 and Alku et al. 2023 refine DNN trackers), on dev voices against the closed-phase reference and the shift probe; (2) **an ensemble member** of labeller v4 (M4), weighted by its frame-level reliability (SEDREAMS' GCI confidence, the fit's stability over adjacent frames). Its weak points to measure first: spurious poles from the short weighted span (seen on a toy voice at 10 kHz), and f0 above about 400 Hz, where it fails as every closed-phase method does. In the baselines as `qcp_fb` and `qcp_fb_published` (2026-10-29). *First run (2026-10-29, `reports/suite/baselines_dev_qcp.md`, Hillenbrand dev)*: frame by frame, labelling the k-th stable root Fk, QCP-FB is poor beyond men's F1: `qcp_fb` F1 / F2 / F3 MAE 72 / 444 / 557 Hz (men's DR 98 / 89 / 81 %, women's 80 / 52 / 40 %); `qcp_fb_published` 79 / 708 / 769. The F2 / F3 failures are assignment errors (one spurious pole shifts every label), not resonance errors: the papers pick peaks and track them (dynamic programming) or snap a DNN's estimates to the nearest peak. So QCP-FB is a refinement component, not a tracker on its own; the refinement test (1) above sidesteps assignment entirely. *FastTrack seeds snapped to QCP-FB (2026-10-29, `reports/suite/baselines_dev_snap.md`, Hillenbrand dev, report-only)*: F1 MAE 17 Hz (FastTrack) → 30 Hz with any of the three seeds (raw, median + zero-phase leaky integrator at α 0.15, FastTrack's DCT fit); F2 45 → 58, F3 53 → 70. The level improves (FastTrack reads men's and women's F1 about +8 to +10 Hz above Hillenbrand's labels; the snap moves it by a median −11 / −9 Hz, to −4.6 / −2.8), but the frame-to-frame scatter of QCP-FB's peaks (snap − FastTrack F1 interquartile range about ±25 Hz) outweighs it; girls move up (+16). Smoothing the seed changes 1–6 % of frames: the jitter is in QCP-FB's peaks, not the seed. Next variants (report-only, α fixed): smooth after the snap; snap the token's median. Caveat: Hillenbrand's labels are LPC with hand checking, so LPC trackers agree with them partly by construction.

- **Uniform-scale ("tape speed") augmentation of the resynthesised real voices** (2026-10-30; Hannah, after D-050 probe 11). - **Reversed clips in training** (2026-10-30; Hannah; parked, low priority). ## Synthesiser

Gathered 2026-10-29 (Hannah, after D-045 to D-047): D-045 found real and synthetic frames perfectly separable in teacher v3's features; D-047 found the aspiration *level* does not move teacher v3's F1 bias on renders. So the open questions are what the renders lack and whether any of it matters for F1. References for the noise entries: `docs/papers/literature.md`, "Aspiration noise, aperiodicity and periodic/aperiodic decomposition". Order: measure first (the first three entries), then change the synthesiser, one arm at a time, each pre-registered as a data arm.

- **Band periodic fraction, renders against real voices** (the cheap first check). SoFilNet's aperiodicity target (Meka et al. 2026, eqs 11–12: per-band autocorrelation at the frame's own period, corrected for the window's taper; bands 0–1, 1–2, 2–4, 4–6, 6–8 kHz) is fully specified, so it needs no model. Compute it on f0-matched train_v2 renders and on real training-pool voices (Zeroth test, speechocean762 adults; never an evaluation set, since this would shape synthesis priors), per band and sex. Our aspiration has one fixed shape relative to the harmonics (differentiated white noise, +6 dB/octave, against the LF source's falling harmonics), so its aperiodicity rises with frequency in one fixed way; real voices' profile varies with phone class, speaker and voice quality. About an hour of CPU. If the profiles agree, the next two entries drop in priority.
- **Discriminator probes: what makes a frame look real** (D-045 follow-up). Dimension rankings are ill-posed under perfect separation, so probe through the input: (1) band occlusion of real and synthetic audio (rules out recording-chain cues first: bandwidth, room, microphone, background noise); (2) D_h's real-ness against each synthetic clip's known generator settings (Rd, `hnr_db`, jitter, shimmer, tube or cascade, length, nasal and subglottal extras, coupling, channel), so the settings that make renders look real point at what the synthesiser lacks; (3) where the resynthesised training clips (real contours, our source and tract) fall; (4) gradient maps of real-ness over the VQT input, confirmed by occlusion; (5) controlled render variants for causal checks (D-047's six arms already exist). Also usable later as a realism score for any new synthetic set.
- **Pre-filter noise from recordings.** SEDREAMS GCIs (`vocal_analysis/eval/qcp.py`) → inverse filtering (QCP-FB or closed-phase LPC) → periodic/aperiodic split (Jackson & Shadle's pitch-scaled harmonic filter, or Yegnanarayana et al.'s iterative residual decomposition) → the noise's spectral slope before the tract, its level per band against the harmonics, and its modulation over the glottal cycle (GCI-synchronous envelope averages). **Validate on renders first**, where the aspiration component is known (`generate(..., keep_components=True)`): the pipeline must recover a known noise's shape and modulation before it is trusted on real voices. Training-pool voices only (no EGG there; the EGG corpora are evaluation sets). Risks: inverse-filter errors leak into the noise estimate, most near F1; room and microphone noise is not glottal, but its lack of glottal-cycle modulation separates it partly.
- **Noise model upgrades**, decided by the measurements above:
  - *Timing*: a second noise peak at closure (Mehta & Quatieri found real vowels' noise peaks in the open phase and near closure; ours is 0.25 + sin² over the open phase only); the modulation depth drawn per clip rather than fixed.
  - *Shape*: a drawn spectral tilt for the pre-filter noise, or per-band levels (a STRAIGHT/WORLD-style aperiodicity profile drawn per clip), instead of one fixed +6 dB/octave shape.
  - *Data-driven*: Drugman & Dutoit's deterministic plus stochastic model, fitted to training-pool residuals, as a source of realistic noise envelopes and spectra.
  - *Variation in time*: breathiness changes within a sentence (Klatt & Klatt 1990), but `hnr_db` is one value per clip; let it wander (see contours below).
- **Realistic contours in the pure synthetic set, beyond f0** (Hannah). Real f0 contours are already in `roadmap.md` (Later: borrowed from the training pools, randomly transposed; D-034's harmonic staircase argues for it). The same applies to the other per-clip constants or hand-made trajectories: loudness (our `loudness_sd_db` wander against real level contours), aperiodicity / HNR (one value per clip today), Rd / voice quality (`rd_wander_sd`), vibrato and jitter as measured on VocalSet rather than drawn. Sources: teacher v3's tracks and a WORLD/D4C or Praat analysis of training-pool recordings (never the evaluation sets), drawn jointly per borrowed utterance so their correlations survive (louder → more harmonic, phrase-final → breathier and lower). Resynthesis already carries real f0 and formant contours with our source; this brings real dynamics to clips whose tract is drawn freely, so the label space stays wide.
- **A list of resynthesised clips that keep too much of the real tract** (2026-10-29; Hannah, after D-049). The resynthesis source is the recording inverse-filtered with a tracker's formants; `resynth.render.source_qc` fits β (`tract_left`), the share of the *tracked* tract still in the source (0 = removed, 1 = untouched, negative = over-removed), and clips with median |β| > 0.5 are rejected at build (`max_tract_left`). Accepted clips keep their median β (`flat/clip_tract_left.npy`) and training down-weights them by it; rejected clips are counted by reason in each store's `index.json` but not listed (Zeroth val: 24 rejections, all for too few voiced frames).
  - *The list*: per store, every clip's median β and ripple (`ripple_db`, what the fit leaves: tract errors the tracks don't describe), with the source file, plus the rejected clips re-run through `source_qc` so their β is recorded too.
  - *Why it matters for the F1 overread*: D-049 found no bias with the real source and an exact synthetic tract, which points at the real tract. Clips where more of the real tract survives are a graded dose of it with exact labels for the synthetic part. If teacher v3's F1 error rises with positive β, the real tract's residue carries the overread: a direct test of the register's main suspect. D-049's per-clip errors are cached (`runs/d049/*.json`), so the first look is seconds of CPU (post hoc on D-049, so register it as its own entry before reading it).
  - *Caveat*: β measures the residue of the *tracked* tract. Where the tracker misplaced F1, the real F1's residue is not what β fits; `ripple_db` and a residual peak search near the label's F1 would catch it. So the dose test (its own entry, on D-049's cached per-clip errors) is one-sided: a rising slope implicates the real tract, a flat one rules out little, since the residue β cannot see (where the tracker is wrong) is the part that matters most. Tracking also worsens at high f0, so β and the overread can share a cause unrelated to the tract: control f0 and sex, report within sex × f0 bins. Held (Hannah, 2026-10-29): not registered for now.
- **Leaky closure** (D-026's Holmberg gap was judged suspect: every model read F1 low under it, which real breathy voices don't show; D-043 measured the closed-phase reference's drift with a gap). - **Widen the bandwidth prior towards real voices** (2026-10-30; after D-050 probes 1, 7, 11 and 12). Real voices' B1 / B2 read 1.4–1.8 × the prior (probe 1) from models whose bandwidth estimates are compressed towards it (follow 0.3–0.8, probes 7 and 11), so real bandwidths are likely wider still. Wider training bandwidths make the envelope peak and the pole disagree more often, the case the formant heads need to learn for the 1 kHz F1 lead (`f1_bias_register.md`, Open), and give the bandwidth heads a wider range. Probe 12 confirmed that the bandwidth heads mostly read the prior off the formants and f0 (weight 0.54–0.85 on the prior, 0.11–0.42 on the truth, D-050), so widening the prior alone will not make them measure. - **A Kelly–Lochbaum waveguide with glottal coupling, ported from voice-dataset-labeler** (2026-10-30; Hannah). Today every synthetic clip, tube or not, comes out of our resonator cascade: the tube mode only computes the poles. A time-domain KL waveguide is a second acoustic path with exact labels, and with a coupled glottis it produces source–tract interaction physically: the F1 register's main suspect, where D-026's quasi-static coupling overshot and D-052 found real voices' open phases longer than ours. OPENGLOT Repository II (Story's model with interaction) showed the overread growing with the glottal gap, but it has 96 sustained vowels and is evaluation only; a KL set of our own could be varied, dynamic and trainable.
  - *Existing code*: `external/voice-dataset-labeler`, `synth/kl.py` (403 lines, MIT, Hannah's) on `origin/main`, spec `KL_SYNTH_SPEC.md` on `origin/feat_bayesian_vtl`. Scattering loop, coupled glottal boundary (Bernoulli flow against the reflected pressure, with an uncoupled switch), glottal area waveform with open quotient, jitter and shimmer. Its own suite (run with `synth/tests` on the path, not pytest): 20 of 25 checks pass, all the coupling ones included.
  - *Why it failed (2026-10-30, checked on an exported copy; the submodule was not touched)*: **an algebra slip in the spec's GL-3.** Solving `Ug = A₀(p⁺₀ − p⁻₀)/(ρc)` gives `p⁺₀ = ρc·Ug/A₀ + p⁻₀`; the spec and code halve both terms, so a closed glottis reflects only half the wave. That leaks energy at every closure (R12) and makes every resonance about 127 Hz wide, so the radiated spectrum's +6 dB/oct tilt pushes the spectral peaks up by about B²/4f (predicted +16 / +5.4 / +3.2 Hz, observed +17.6 / +5.9 / +3.9: R11, R14). - **Laryngeal mechanisms M0-M3 in synthesis** (2026-11-04; Hannah). Today every source is one LF / Rd family (D-026's interactive source aside), which is roughly modal chest voice (M1). Real voices, and singers especially (VocalSet), also use M0 (vocal fry / pulse: very low, often irregular f0 with long closed phases, double or triple pulsing and subharmonics), M2 (falsetto / head: thin folds, large open quotient, weak or no full closure, steep spectral tilt, more aspiration) and M3 (whistle register: very high f0, nearly sinusoidal source, little closure; one or two harmonics per formant region at most). Each changes what the formant evidence looks like: M0's long closed phases and sparse pulses help closed-phase analysis but break periodicity assumptions; M2's weak closure is the leaky-closure question (D-074 to D-078) in its strongest form; M3 leaves F1 essentially unobservable. Sketch: a mechanism draw per clip (or per phrase, with transitions and the M1-M2 break as an f0-dependent switch with hysteresis), each mechanism its own region of LF / Rd parameters, closed quotient, gap and noise level, with M0 adding pulse-train irregularity (jitter, shimmer, subharmonic period doubling) and M3 a near-sinusoidal source. Labels stay the tract's; where a mechanism leaves a formant unobservable that is the observability question (Later in `roadmap.md`). Priors from the literature (Roubeau, Henrich, Castellengo on mechanisms; EGG-derived closed quotients by mechanism) and from training-pool singing, never from evaluation sets. A first check before any arm: whether v3.1's errors on VocalSet val differ by technique label (VocalSet labels fry, falsetto, belt and so on), which says which mechanisms the model already handles.
  - *Data to look for* (Hannah): recordings with each mechanism labelled, for resynthesis (real sources per mechanism) and for checking the synthetic ones; ideally with EGG, and ingressive (inhaled) phonation too, which no current training corpus has. Licence rules as for any corpus (no NC / SA for training; evaluation-only sets never fit priors). Ingressive phonation in synthesis would need reversed transglottal flow: whether the KL synthesiser's glottal model handles that is to be checked; the cascade has no flow to reverse.
- **Fitting the sampler's dynamics from teacher tracks** (2026-10-30; Hannah asked about a generative track model). A model trained to generate the teacher's tracks would inherit its biases (women's F1 slightly high, bandwidths read off the formants, D-050 probe 12) and feed them back into training, a loop the label-exact synthesis cannot correct because the bias is in the distribution, not the labels. Resynthesis already drives real sources with teacher tracks. The safer version: fit the parametric sampler's dynamics statistics (transition durations and rates, how fast F2 / F3 move, coarticulation timing) from teacher tracks on the non-gold pools, using only what the teacher measures reliably, never levels it is known to get wrong. D-049 ruled dynamics out as the F1 cause, so the gain would be realism (D-045's separability), not the overread.

## Heads

- **DAC heads on 80 ms means** (2026-10-26). The claim that "acoustic encoders let heads learn the synthetic F1 shortcut" (D-033 part 5, logged as untested) rests on one ASR encoder (Parakeet, 80 ms frames) against one codec (DAC, about 12 ms). Frame rate is confounded with the objective. Training DAC heads on DAC features averaged to 80 ms (six seeds, the existing DAC caches, about 1 h) would separate them. If the overread mostly goes away, frame rate explains much of it, and acoustic-target techniques (codec codes, spectral reconstruction) become less suspect. This matters before relying on the claim when choosing encoders or pretraining objectives.
- **H5 initialised, fine-tuned on a student's 10 ms frames with random offsets** (2026-10-26; Hannah, idle curiosity). Expected to land near the matched-noise control, since the H5 sweep found the students carry no more of H5's correction than noise does. If tried, it needs the same fine-tune on Parakeet plus matched noise as its control. *Down-weighted 2026-10-26*: phase-aligned pooling removed v4's phase mismatch without any training (D-034), and the student line is paused (roadmap).
- **Cascade order between the VTL head and the formant heads** (2026-10-27; Hannah). Today the VTL head reads the formant trunk alone, beside the formant heads. Two directions, each with detached inputs (as the observability head takes formant positions), so neither loss can move the other's outputs:
  - *Formants → VTL*: the VTL head also takes F1–F4's expected positions. The planned D-037 candidate probes this on frozen features first. Risk: the formants' own f0 bias (F1 reads high in high voices) reaching the length estimate, the VTL-vs-f0 guard D-035 failed.
  - *VTL → formants*: the formant heads also take the VTL head's log length and σ, a speaker-size prior. Worth trying only if the VTL head is both confident and accurate on real voices. A prior would help most where the audio says least: F3 / F4, and frames the observability head marks as weakly observed. Risk: a confident but wrong length (or one with f0 drift) dragging every formant with it; formant heads that lean on the prior instead of the evidence.
  - When the joint per-formant head comes back (roadmap, paused), an ablation over the order (none / formants → VTL / VTL → formants), one change at a time, with formant MAE by formant (F3 / F4 especially), closed-phase bias, the invariance probe for both outputs, and the VTL measures.
- **A whole-cycle effective F1 head** (2026-10-29; Hannah, after D-048; `docs/f1_bias_register.md`, "F1's definition"). Our F1 label is the closed-glottis resonance (D-023). In the open phase, coupling to the subglottal system raises and broadens F1, so real voices' spectra carry a cycle-averaged effective resonance, which every reference here measures to some degree. If teacher v3 reads that effective F1 on real voices, part of the overread is a definition gap, not an error.
  - *Label*: per frame, the closed-glottis F1 plus the open-phase shift weighted by the open quotient (the time-average over the cycle; an energy-weighted average as a variant). The generator already draws the shift (`coupled["df"]`, per target, on tube clips with a glottis); cascade clips and clips without coupling have effective = closed. It isn't in the label store, so labels come from regenerating clips (deterministic), or a new store.
  - *Step 1, frozen features*: a head for effective F1 on teacher v3's frozen trunk, beside a refitted closed-glottis head (the paired control, as D-035 / D-037). Measures: synthetic MAE on coupled clips for both labels (can the trunk tell them apart at all?); on PTDB-TUG dev, each head's bias against the closed-phase reference and R. *Reading*: if the effective head's real-voice prediction sits near the closed-glottis head's, the trunk does not separate the two on real voices; if teacher v3's F1 lies nearer the effective head than the closed head on real voices, the definition explains that share. Check first how large the shift is in training data (median OQ × df on women's-range tube clips): if it is a few Hz, the head can't explain +6.
  - *Step 2, end to end*: the effective head trained jointly (a decay branch, as D-036), so the trunk can learn the distinction. **Not gated on step 1** (Hannah): training only for closed-glottis F1 may have removed the distinction from the trunk the heads read, so a frozen probe that cannot separate the two definitions does not show the end-to-end model couldn't. The only precondition is the size check above (the shift in training data must be large enough to matter); step 1 is then context: if the frozen trunk already separates them, end-to-end training is less likely to add much.
  - *Risk*: the coupling shift as synthesised (D-026: the interactive sets overshot at full size) may not match real voices', so the effective label is only as good as the coupling model.
- **Source heads and source inputs: keeping the glottis in the trunk** (2026-11-04; Hannah and Claude, after D-078 / D-079, while D-080 runs). D-079: every trunk, coupled-trained or not, v3.1 included, exposes only about 0.18 of the frame's mean glottal opening (about 0.45 per clip), against 0.75 from the frame's Rd alone, so source information seems to be dropped by the time it reaches the 128-d per-frame vector the heads read. No current head needs it (the f0 comb, built from f0 and voicing alone, gives the trunk harmonic positions at equal height, not their amplitudes). Ideas, ordered by D-080's outcome:
  - *Auxiliary source heads* (if D-080's opening head is learned): Rd and the source spectrum's level per frame need no new labels (`label_rd`, `label_source_h_db` are in every rendered set); open quotient and spectral tilt follow from the LF parameters; jitter and shimmer are drawn per clip (`meta`), so they would need per-frame label plumbing like D-079's opening label. Synthetic supervision only, masked elsewhere, as the opening head. They make the trunk keep (and the formant heads able to use) the source; a natural joint test with the opening head.
  - *Source features at the input* (if the opening is learned but not used, or not learned): hand-computed per-frame features beside the VQT, as the comb is (energy, H1-H2 from the f0 estimate, spectral tilt, a harmonic-amplitude comb read at the harmonics). They guarantee the information enters the trunk, not that it survives to the output, so they address the other half of the problem from heads.
  - *Within-clip vowel contrast* (Hannah): through F1 the opening is identifiable only across vowels that share a glottis (each shifts by its own df x <a>); a clip spans several (F1 ~980 cents, <a> nearly constant) but short training crops may not. D-080 reports R² by crop length and F1 spread; if the contrast route matters, longer crops or a clip-level source state (a per-clip latent the frame heads read) are the follow-ups.
  - *The invariance arm* (D-079 / D-080 design, deferred): an adversary keeping F1 (or vowel) information out of the opening head, so it cannot stand in for the vowel-dependent offset.
  - *Capacity caveat* (Hannah, 2026-11-04): every extra head competes for the same 128 numbers per frame. The formant guard catches a cost but not its cause; if heads crowd each other, widen the per-frame vector (or give the source heads their own projection off the encoder) as a controlled arm, not alongside the heads. D-079's small trunks (1.9 M parameters, 3500 steps) add capacity and training-length confounds of their own; v3.1 (4.2 M, 20000 steps) at the same level speaks against them explaining the common level, not against them hiding a coupled-training effect.
- **Energy in the F1–F2 valley as F1 evidence** (2026-10-30; Hannah parked it after D-050 probe 3). A 1 kHz peak moves F1 by about ±12 cents in every model, and cutting it lowers the F1 error (`docs/f1_bias_register.md`, Open). Test: on resynthesised speech (probe 1's renderer, exact labels), add a fixed peak or a broad shelf between F1 and F2 at several levels and positions, women and men separately; F1 should not move. If it does, compare the valley level of real women's voices with the synthesis prior's. Check the noise floor first (seed spread from probe 3).
- **Bandwidth estimates and context** (2026-10-30; Hannah parked them under roadmap item 10). Bandwidth outputs follow about half of a real change (probe 7), widen with jitter (probe 6: B2 up to +24 cents in v3.1, +63 in v1) and with silence around the clip (probe 4: B1 +8 to +30 cents for 2 s each side), though the labels do not move. Calibrating the bandwidth heads' σ (item 10) comes first; a fix to the estimates themselves would be its own decision.

## Back end and outcomes

- **VTL-normalised formants as an output** (2026-10-27; Hannah, from earlier work where VTL-normalised formants classified vowels better). The formants scaled to a reference length (F × L / L_ref, a shift in log frequency) keep mostly the vowel's shape. Unlike Lobanov or Nearey normalisation, which need a speaker's whole vowel inventory, a VTL estimate works from one recording, which is how outcome recordings arrive. Use the speaker's length from the recording as a whole (or a leave-one-out over its tokens), never the same frame's estimate, which can absorb the vowel itself (D-035's vowel-spread measure).
- **Splitting a formant change into size and shape** (2026-10-27; for the long-term surgical-outcomes aim). A before / after change in formants mixes a change in vocal-tract length (e.g. a raised or smaller larynx) with a change in articulation and resonance strategy. The VTL change and the change in VTL-normalised formants separate them. Worth doing once a VTL estimate passes on real voices, with its uncertainty carried through, since a small length change is easily within noise.

## Evaluation: invariance and equivariance probes

Hannah (2026-10-28): which perturbations should leave an output unchanged, and which should move it by a known amount, is one of the main tests separating a research-grade model from a hobbyist one. Today only the f0 probe exists (synthetic, invariance only). Each probe reports a slope against the imposed change with a shuffle null (equivariance: slope 1; invariance: slope 0), per output (F1–F4, bandwidths, VTL), and whether σ grows instead of the estimate drifting. Where possible each runs twice: on the synthetic probe (exact truth) and on real voices.

- **Counterfactual resynthesis** (the first to build): real voices from a non-gold dev pool, resynthesised unchanged / f0 only / tract length only (all formants scaled by one factor) / both / voice quality only. Equivariance for length and formants under scaling, invariance under f0 and quality. Check first that resynthesis at a scaled length adds no artefacts of its own. Also the check to run on any future consented before/after data before trusting a head on it.
- **Single-formant shift** (resynthesis, one formant moved): the moved formant should follow; the others and VTL should barely move. Measures cross-talk, and how far the cascade leans on priors rather than evidence.
- **Voice quality**: breathiness (aspiration, open quotient / Rd), creak, roughness (jitter, shimmer), falsetto. Post-operative and pathological voices differ here.
- **Vocal effort**: spectral tilt changes with level; formants and length should not.
- **Channel** (no resynthesis needed: degrade real gold-set audio and pair against the clean result, e.g. Hillenbrand MAE): microphone frequency responses, telephone band-limiting (F4 unobservable: σ should grow, not bias), codecs and bitrates (the USC MRI audio is AAC), reverberation, additive noise by SNR (white, babble, café), denoiser artefacts (musical noise, as in the MRI audio).
- **Timing**: speaking rate (time-stretch), crop position and context length, sub-hop offsets, polarity inversion.
- **Time reversal** (frame-level outputs): a frame's spectrum is unchanged by reversal, so a frame-local estimate should be too. A large change shows reliance on temporal or linguistic context.
  - *With a change of speaker* (Hannah): on A-then-B joins, forward against reversed per frame, by distance from the join. Reversal swaps which side of the join gets the other speaker as context, so the difference profile measures leakage across the change and how far it reaches. It is paired on identical frame spectra, so each frame's own estimation error cancels, and smaller leakage is detectable than against references.
  - *Care*: dry audio only (reverberation tails become pre-echo); exact frame mapping (pad to a whole number of hops so frame centres coincide); the analysis windows must be symmetric for the magnitudes to match. Context use within one speaker is legitimate (a speaker-level length *should* pool frames), so reversal alone flags context use; the speaker change is what isolates the harmful kind.
- **Content**: vowel spread of VTL (already measured), across languages (ArtSpeechMRIfr, Zeroth), nasalisation (added pole-zero pairs: F1 should not jump).
- **A quick tier of the probe suite for iteration** (2026-10-30; Hannah). The full suite stays the release and regression baseline: run once per released or decided model and kept with its reports. The channel probe alone takes about 50 min per model on a shared GPU. A fixed stratified subset for iterating: one Hillenbrand file per speaker group and vowel, 5 PTDB-TUG files per speaker, one level per condition family (opus, telephone, the 1 kHz EQ pair, reverberation 0.4 s, 10 dB white and babble noise); the other probes at 2 items per speaker. About 2–3 min per model. It flags only: its own thresholds come from the seed spread measured on the subset, and a flag is confirmed on the full suite before it counts.
