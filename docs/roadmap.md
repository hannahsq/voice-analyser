## Speed and machine notes

AMR streams through one long-lived ffmpeg per bitrate per loader worker (loader 829–1036 s/s); 4 workers for S2; validation cached on the GPU; closed phase on the GPU (`add_model.py`, `VA_DEVICE=cuda`, identical to CPU); `torch.compile` and HIP graph capture parked (no Triton on ROCm under Windows; capture aborts in hipBLASLt); priority opt-in (`VA_PRIORITY`), no measurable gain; under CPU load S2 loses 15–36 %, so keep measures off the CPU during training. Open: a batched GPU eval suite for the paired measures and the probe (closed phase done); profiling the training step needs ablation timing, since the ROCm profiler's GPU times are unreliable here. Machine notes: CPU boost on; CPPC core order 7, 8, 5, 4, 1, 6, 3, 2, then 11, 12, 13, 15, 10, 16, 9, 14 (HWiNFO numbering from 1); the main training thread runs on cores 6–7 (Windows numbering) and GPU use follows it.

## Training recipe experiments

Done (D-029, D-030): on S2, learning rate ×8 (8e-3), then Muon 8e-3 for the non-head linear weights + Adan 4e-2 for the rest; warmup 250 and decay over 20 % were withdrawn at that rate; schedule-free AdamW worse; no optimiser halves the steps. The full-size check is D-031.

Its test is therefore (1) no harm on clean dev (the usual guard-rails) and (2) improvement on **noisy copies of the dev gold sets**: gold audio mixed with a held-out slice of each noise / RIR source (reserved before any training use, never trained on) at fixed SNRs, evaluation only. The noise itself is varied per draw: random offset and length, gain, spectral tilt / EQ, speed perturbation (about ±10 %), two or three layered sources, and convolution with a real RIR so the noise shares the voice's room; the existing chain (filters, telephone band, codecs) then acts on voice and noise together. *Order fix (Hannah, 2026-10-23)*: `apply_channel` today runs voice → RIR → high-pass → EQ → **+ dry noise** → … → codec, so noise never shares the room or the microphone's filtering. The noise's room is the voice's room with its own source position: for synthetic RIRs a second draw with the same T60 and band decay, lower direct-to-reverberant ratio (noise sources sit farther away); for real RIR sets (RWCP, AIR) another position in the same room. Point-source noises get the room; diffuse / ambient recordings (RIRS_NOISES' isotropic noises, café scenes) already carry one and are not reverberated again. *Spatial scenes (Hannah, 2026-10-23)*: beyond a second RIR draw, simulate the scene: **pyroomacoustics** (MIT, PyPI; image-source method with ray tracing for the tail) gives each source (the voice, each noise) its own physically consistent RIR at one microphone from a drawn shoebox room (size, wall absorption), microphone and source positions, so distance, direct-to-reverberant ratio and delays follow from geometry; with **talker directivity** (a voice facing away loses its highs, changing tilt above about 2 kHz and so F3 / F4 observability, which ties into D-027's label). Moving sources (head turns, passing traffic) by switching RIRs over time, later. Computed CPU-side in loader workers, RIRs batched and cached per room. Not gpuRIR (AGPL). *Rendered café hubbub (Hannah, 2026-10-23; method borrowed from her `hannahsq/umbrella-rain`)*: tables of 2–4 talkers drawn from our connected-speech pools (LibriTTS-R, Common Voice, MLS, Zeroth, speechocean762, Samrómur; never the target speaker), each table taking turns with conversational gaps and overlaps; tables on log-spaced distance rings (umbrella-rain's bed layer: area grows as ρ², intensity falls as 1/r², so each ring carries similar power: many quiet far talkers, a few near ones); per-source fractional delay and 1/r gain (`add_fractional`); talker directivity from the Brown–Duda rigid-sphere head shadow used in reverse (the head as radiator); a slow correlated level envelope (the gust model) for the room's ebb and flow; clatter as modal grains (umbrella-rain's tick / Minnaert "plink" approach: cups, cutlery, glass), so synthesised, licence-free; recorded beds (espresso hiss, room tone) from MUSAN noise and FSD50K CC0 / CC BY clips (MUSAN's music only where its per-file licence is clean). - **Device processing: automatic gain control** (Hannah, 2026-10-24). The channel model's level is one static gain per clip (`gain_db`) followed by clipping and the codec; nearly every phone, laptop and video-call path also runs AGC before its encoder, so the consented outcome recordings will mostly have been through one. Model it as one AGC on the **final mix** (room → noise / scene mix → AGC → clipping → codec, behind a channel-config switch, off by default), since a real AGC derives its gain from whatever reaches the microphone: on a clean recording it evens out the speaker's own level; with a scene, a loud event (a motorcycle passing) makes it duck the voice with it, and the noise floor pumps back up in the gaps. It belongs to the channel model, not to `random-cafe` (it is the device, not the scene); `random-cafe` supplies the events. Parameters drawn per clip: attack (about 1–20 ms), release (50 ms – 2 s), target level, maximum gain (up to about 20–30 dB), ratio and knee, plus a slow adaptive-digital-gain mode (WebRTC AGC2-like). Cheap: an attack/release envelope follower on 1 ms blocks, interpolated. Labels are level-independent. Where it could hurt: gain moving within the VQT's long low-frequency windows (low bins see an averaged gain, high bins the current one: a transient tilt change in the F1 region), fast attack modulating within a glottal period at high f0 (sidebands, harmonics broadened, so bandwidths), and pumping noise between words (voicing). **Two steps**: (1) an evaluation-only stress test, no training: the dev gold sets through a few AGC presets (slow digital gain; fast phone compressor; either ducking under a `random-cafe` scene), teacher v3 scored as paired differences against the unprocessed audio (F1 bias, bandwidths, voicing); (2) only if that shows harm, a pre-registered augmentation arm (AGC with probability p, nothing else changed). The stress set stays as a robustness check either way.
- **Device processing: noise, echo and reverberation suppression** (Hannah, 2026-10-24). Probably more destructive than AGC, since it reshapes the spectrum rather than scaling it: spectral-subtraction / Wiener / neural noise suppressors notch and smear harmonics and can eat weak high formants (F3 / F4 observability, D-027); residual-echo suppression (the non-linear stage after acoustic echo cancellation) gates and attenuates onsets and tails, and in an acoustically reflective room it treats the voice's own reverberant tail as echo; dereverberation does the same deliberately. Many devices **beamform** with two or more microphones first, which needs the spatial scene: `random-cafe` / pyroomacoustics rendering the voice and each noise source to a small microphone array (phone, laptop, headset geometries) rather than one microphone, then the beamformer, then single-channel suppression, AGC and the codec. Real devices' processing is proprietary, so use open stand-ins as proxies, licence and build to check before use (candidates: WebRTC's audio processing module (BSD: AEC3, noise suppression, AGC2), SpeexDSP's preprocessor (BSD), RNNoise (BSD), and a classical delay-and-sum / MVDR beamformer written here). Same two steps as AGC: an evaluation-only stress test first (dev gold sets through each processor, with and without a scene and a reflective room; teacher v3 paired against the unprocessed audio), then, only on demonstrated harm, a pre-registered augmentation arm. Order with AGC: beamformer → echo / noise suppression → AGC → codec, as in typical voice-processing pipelines.
