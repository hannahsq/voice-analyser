Each entry says what was read: **full text** (read and checked), **abstract** (abstract or listing only: claims unverified), or **code** (the authors' implementation read as a specification). Started 2026-10-28 (Hannah: "read and reference the literature for this one; QCP-FB might not even be state of the art any more").

## Closed-phase and quasi-closed-phase linear prediction

- **Alku (1992)**, *Glottal wave analysis with pitch synchronous iterative adaptive inverse filtering* (IAIF). Separates the glottal source and the tract without an EGG by alternating low- and high-order LP fits. The baseline QCP improves on. *Not yet read.*
- **Alku, Pohjalainen, Vainio, Laukkanen & Story (2013)**, JASA, formant estimation from high-pitched vowels with weighted LP (the AME weighting function). Physical-model synthetic vowels. *Not yet read.*
- **Airaksinen, Raitio, Story & Alku (2014)**, *Quasi closed phase glottal inverse filtering analysis with weighted linear prediction*, IEEE/ACM TASLP 22(3), 596–607, doi:10.1109/TASLP.2013.2294585. Introduces QCP: weighted LP whose weight function emphasises the closed phase and attenuates the main excitation; closures (GCIs) estimated from speech, no EGG; evaluated on LF and physical-model synthetic vowels at low and high f0. *Abstract.*
- **Gowda, Airaksinen & Alku (2017)**, *Quasi-closed phase forward-backward linear prediction analysis of speech for accurate formant detection and estimation*, JASA 142(3), 1542–1550, doi:10.1121/1.5001512. **Full text.**
  - Method: minimise F = Σ w_n (x_n + Σ_k a_k x_{n−k})² + Σ w_n (x_n + Σ_k a_k x_{n+k})², one coefficient set for forward and backward prediction, covariance range (QCP-FBCOV). Normal equations Σ_k d_{i,k} a_k = −d_{i,0} with d_{i,k} = Σ w_n x_{n−i} x_{n−k} + Σ w_n x_{n+i} x_{n+k}.
  - Weights: QCP function with position quotient 0.05, duration quotient 0.7, ramp 7 samples (8 kHz), minimum 10⁻⁵; GCIs from SEDREAMS (Drugman et al. 2012). Order 13 at 8 kHz, 30 ms rectangular frames, pre-emphasis 1 − 0.97 z⁻¹; peaks by convolving the spectrum with a 100 Hz Gaussian-derivative window.
  - Evidence: physical-model vowels (adult male, female, 5-year-old child; f0 100–450 Hz; 10 kHz) and VTR-TIMIT test (192 utterances, detection rate only, since VTR's labels are not ground truth). Forward-backward gives more equations per short closed phase and less line splitting; gains largest for women.
  - Stated limitation: depends on the GCI estimates; robustness to GCI errors under degradation not studied.
- **Gowda, Kadiri, Story & Alku (2020)**, *Time-varying quasi-closed-phase analysis for accurate formant tracking in speech signals* (TVQCP), IEEE/ACM TASLP 28, 1901–1914; arXiv 2308.16540. **Full text (method section).** QCP weights inside time-varying LP (polynomial coefficient trajectories over 200 ms blocks, L1 or L2 error); pitch-adaptive QCP parameters from a grid search on about 65,000 LF vowels (position quotient 0.05, ramp 0.375 ms, duration quotient 0.5–0.9, 0.8 used). Simulated child speech up to f0 600 Hz.
  - **Code**: github.com/njaygowda/ftrack (Apache-2.0, MATLAB; uses the GLOAT toolbox, BSD-style licence, for SEDREAMS and SRH pitch). `qcp_wt`: per cycle, weight 1 from GCI + PQ·T0 for DQ·T0 samples, linear ramps of Nramp samples at each end, the minimum elsewhere. Defaults there: DQ 0.7, PQ 0.05, Nramp 3, minimum 10⁻⁵, order 8, 8 kHz.
- **Gowda, Bollepalli, Kadiri & Alku (2021)**, *Formant tracking using quasi-closed phase forward-backward linear prediction analysis and deep neural networks*, IEEE Access; arXiv 2201.01525. DNN formant estimates refined frame by frame with the nearest QCP-FB spectral peak; reports error reductions of 29 / 48 / 35 % (F1–F3) against Wavesurfer on VTR. *Abstract.*
- **Alku, Kadiri & Gowda (2023)**, *Refining a deep learning-based formant tracker using linear prediction methods*, Computer Speech and Language 81, 101515; arXiv 2308.09051. DeepFormants refined with LP-COV or QCP-FB peaks (no retraining); QCP-FB best on VTR and noisy VTR. *Abstract.*

## Other formant trackers and evaluation

- **Deng et al. (2006)**, VTR-TIMIT formant database (semi-automatic, hand-corrected F1–F3). The field's usual benchmark. **It is built on TIMIT, so we cannot use it without an LDC licence** (AGENTS.md: no unlicensed TIMIT); comparisons with the papers above must be by method on our own references, not by their VTR numbers.
- **Mehta, Rudoy & Wolfe (2012)**, KARMA: Kalman-based ARMA formant and antiformant tracking; arXiv 1107.0076. Notes VTR's labelling errors. *Abstract.*
- **Dissen, Goldberger & Keshet (2019)**, *Formant estimation and tracking: a deep learning approach* (DeepFormants), JASA; code github.com/MLSpeech/DeepFormants. *Not yet read.*
- **Story & Bunton**, spectral filtering for formant measurement in children's speech (PubMed 26855461; POMA 23, 060002). Cepstral smoothing aimed at f0 250–600 Hz. *Not yet read.*
- **Mikusová (2021)**, TU Wien thesis: CNN estimation of tract resonances of synthesised high-pitched vowels. *Listing only.*
- **Meka, Ramesh & Kodukula (2026)**, *Self-supervised source-filter model of speech production* (SoFilNet), arXiv 2610.18120 (13 Oct 2026). Differentiable source-filter network trained by reconstruction only (ordered formant poles, extra poles, zeros); reports the lowest mean formant error on VTR among compared methods, supervised trackers included, and PTDB-TUG f0 as good as the best classical tracker. *Read in full (2026-10-29, v1)*. Trained on the TIMIT training set (no labels), so its weights derive from LDC-licensed data: whether we may run them, as a baseline only, is Hannah's call under the TIMIT rule. Code and weights not released; audio samples at nani-meka.github.io/sofilnet-samples. Per frame (10 ms, 16 kHz, 25 ms window): F0 (sigmoid-bounded to 60-400 Hz), voicing, aperiodicity in five bands (0-1, 1-2, 2-4, 4-6, 6-8 kHz); 4 ordered formants (stick-breaking with soft tanh bounds, ceilings 1.5 / 3 / 4 / 5 kHz, bandwidths up to 600-750 Hz), 2 high poles, 2 broad extra poles (bandwidth > 1 kHz), 2 zeros (250 Hz-4 kHz). *Against our synthesiser*: its source is a flat harmonic comb plus stationary noise mixed by power, with no glottal pulse shape, no glottis (so no closed/open phase, coupling or subglottal system) and the spectral slope moved into the filter, as in LPC. Its F1 is therefore the effective envelope peak, coupling and tilt included, not our closed-glottis target (D-023); the authors call F1 its weakest formant (VTR vowel MAE 53 Hz, all-speech RMSE 136 Hz, behind KARMA's 114). The tract side is close to ours (conjugate pole-pair cascade, nasal-type zeros), and the zeros carry its F2 / F3 gains (without them F3's error doubles); subglottal pairs on vowels would be absorbed by its spare zeros and broad poles. Its per-band aperiodicity is something our one-HNR-per-clip source lacks (D-047's limits). *Training*: reconstruction of real speech (Adam 2e-4, cosine to 1 %, 1000 epochs, noise added to the input only, final checkpoint, no labelled selection), with targets against per-bin blurring (band periodic fraction, spectral contrast, envelope extrema), F0 pseudo-labels from autocorrelation refined on its own inverse-filter residual (HuBERT-style), a log-track smoothness penalty and a multi-resolution gated encoder. *Comparable here*: PTDB-TUG f0 (all 20 speakers, RPA 84.4 %, FFE 5.06 %; we would score dev only).

## Direct resonance measurement (high f0)

- **Joliveau, Smith & Wolfe (2004)**, broadband acoustic excitation at the lips during singing: measures tract resonances directly, independent of the voice's harmonics. The only fair reference for sopranos (D-043 addendum: closed-phase F1 fails from about 450 Hz). *Not yet read.*

## Aspiration noise, aperiodicity and periodic/aperiodic decomposition (potential references; 2026-10-29, after D-047)

Gathered when D-047 found that aspiration *level* does not move teacher v3's F1 bias on renders, leaving the noise's spectral shape and timing open. Known from abstracts and summaries only unless marked; none read in full yet.

- **Klatt & Klatt (1990)**, *Analysis, synthesis, and perception of voice quality variations among female and male talkers*, JASA 87, 820–857 (doi 10.1121/1.398894; [PDF](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/david/ma_ssp/doc/Klatt-1990-JAS000820.pdf)). KLSYN88; aspiration noise modulated by the voicing period. Breathiness cues: the fundamental's relative amplitude, aspiration noise, lower-formant bandwidths, tracheal pole-zero pairs; perceptually aspiration noise mattered most. Women breathier on average, with large individual differences.
- **Mehta & Quatieri (2005, WASPAA; 2006, Interspeech)** and **Mehta (2006, MIT thesis)**, *Aspiration noise during phonation: synthesis, analysis, and pitch-scale modification* ([thesis](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/47819); [Interspeech 2006](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2006/mehta06_interspeech.html); [WASPAA 2005](https://www.ll.mit.edu/r-d/publications/synthesis-analysis-and-pitch-modification-breathy-vowel)). Noise modulated by the glottal flow; in real vowels the noise peaks with the open phase and near closure. Our modulation (0.25 floor, sin² over the open phase) has no closure peak.
- **Jackson & Shadle (2000)**, *Frication noise modulated by voicing, as revealed by pitch-scaled decomposition*, JASA ([preprint](https://personalpages.surrey.ac.uk/p.jackson/pub/jasa00/JacksonShadle_JASA00_preprint.pdf)); **Jackson & Shadle (2001)**, *Pitch-scaled estimation of simultaneous voiced and turbulence-noise components in speech*, IEEE TSAP ([PDF](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/259158/1/ieeesap01.pdf)); Jackson's PhD thesis (2000, Southampton). The pitch-scaled harmonic filter (PSHF): a window of a whole number of periods splits periodic from aperiodic; about 5 dB gain on synthetic tests. The likely decomposition for a pre-filter noise analysis.
- **Yegnanarayana, d'Alessandro & Darsinos (1998)**, *An iterative algorithm for decomposition of speech signals into periodic and aperiodic components*, IEEE TSAP 6(1), 1–11 ([PDF](https://groupeaa.lisn.upsaclay.fr/_media/membres:cda:ieee-cda-1998-algo.pdf)); companion *Effectiveness of a periodic and aperiodic decomposition method for analysis of voice sources* ([PDF](https://groupeaa.lisn.upsaclay.fr/_media/membres:cda:ieee-cda-1998-effectiveness.pdf)). Decomposes the LPC residual (before the tract filter); evaluated on formant-synthesised signals with noise, jitter and shimmer.
- **Drugman & Dutoit (2012)**, *The deterministic plus stochastic model of the residual signal and its applications*, IEEE TASLP 20(3) ([arXiv 2001.00842](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.00842v1.pdf)); **Drugman, Wilfart & Dutoit (2009)**, Interspeech ([ISCA](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2009/drugman09c_interspeech.html)). Residual = PCA-based deterministic part below a maximum voiced frequency + high-pass noise shaped by an energy envelope within each period, learned per speaker from pitch-synchronous residual frames: a data-driven pre-filter noise model.
- **Morise (2016)**, D4C band-aperiodicity estimation, Speech Communication; WORLD (Morise, Yokomori & Ozawa 2016, IEICE) ([publications](https://www.isc.meiji.ac.jp/~mmorise/world/english/publications.html); [code](https://github.com/mmorise/World), modified BSD). The five-band aperiodicity SoFilNet adopts (via STRAIGHT); usable as a measuring tool.
- **Stylianou (1996, thesis; 2001)**, harmonic plus noise model (HNM): noise above a maximum voiced frequency with a time-domain envelope per period. *From memory, not yet checked.*
- **Meka, Ramesh & Kodukula (2026)**, SoFilNet (above): band periodic fraction (their eqs 11–12) as an aperiodicity target; fully specified, so computable on our renders and real voices without their model.

## What this means here (2026-10-28)

- QCP-FB is implementable from the JASA paper plus the authors' weight code; nothing needs to be guessed. Unlike our closed-phase reference it needs no EGG, so it could give references for Hillenbrand (including children) and other voices without EGG, if it holds up on D-043's grid with GCIs estimated from speech.
- Not clearly state of the art any more: neural trackers refined with QCP-FB (2021, 2023) beat it on VTR, and SoFilNet (2026) claims better still. But a *reference* method needs to be model-driven and independent of our training, which QCP-FB is and the neural trackers are not.
- Our D-043 (closed-phase LPC on physical-model renders over f0, open quotient and closure gap) parallels the synthetic evaluations in Airaksinen et al. (2014) and Gowda et al. (2017); Paper A must cite them and say what D-043 adds (leaky closure, open quotient, the 400–800 Hz breakdown).
